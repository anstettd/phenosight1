DRAFT LEGEND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Figure 4. Response of Mimulus traits to soil water-holding capacity (SWHC)
across the whole experiment. Each cell of the ring is one regression of one trait
on % SWHC at one timepoint within one population group. Radius is time of
experiment, with the centre at the start and each successive ring a later
timepoint; angle is trait, grouped into one 90-degree quadrant per population
group, with the traits in the same order within every quadrant and named at the
outer edge of each wedge. Quadrants are arranged as in Figure S1: California on
the right, Oregon on the left, 2014 in the upper half, 2010 in the lower half.
Fill is the signed strength of the SWHC slope, sign(slope) x -log10(P): green
where the trait rises as the pots get wetter, purple where it falls (i.e. the
trait is higher under drought), and near-white where there is no response in
either direction. Red asterisks mark timepoints at which that trait x group
regression passes a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across timepoints at
FDR < 0.05. The dashed ring at 409.5 h marks the point at which the plants
outgrew the imaging trays and the setup was changed; any step across it is
technical, not biological. Population groups are named along the arc, centred on
each quadrant's own span. Inset in each corner, in the corner its own quadrant
points into, is the underlying regression for one cell: NDVI on % SWHC at 144 h,
the first imaging timepoint of the series, for that group - each point one
replicate at one watering regime, the fitted line coloured by the sign of its
slope on the same convention
as the ring, and R2, P and n given above it. The cell each inset is drawn from is
outlined in red, lies on the red dot-dash ring that carries its timepoint right
round the figure, and sits at the end of a short red radial pointer running out
from the centre; ring and pointer cross on the cell.


TRAIT ABBREVIATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reading clockwise from the top of each quadrant:

PSA       Projected shoot area. Rosette area in the image mask, the size
          read-out. Exported as Roi.Mask and renamed PSA in 0_data_setup.R.
Fv/Fm     Maximum quantum yield of photosystem II, measured after dark
          adaptation. The standard measure of PSII photochemical efficiency.
Fq'/Fm'   Operating (effective) quantum yield of PSII in the light; the
          proportion of absorbed light actually used for photochemistry.
NPQ       Non-photochemical quenching. Dissipation of excess excitation energy
          as heat, referenced to a dark-adapted Fm.
qP        Photochemical quenching coefficient (puddle model); an estimate of the
          proportion of open PSII reaction centres.
qN        Non-photochemical quenching coefficient; the same process as NPQ on a
          0-1 scale.
qL        Fraction of open PSII reaction centres under the lake model; the
          connected-antenna counterpart of qP.
qI        Photoinhibitory quenching; the slowly relaxing component of NPQ,
          associated with photodamage to PSII.
phiNO     Quantum yield of non-regulated energy dissipation, 1/(NPQ+1+qI*Fm/F0).
          Energy lost neither to photochemistry nor to regulated dissipation;
          a high value indicates the plant is failing to cope.
phiNPQ    Quantum yield of regulated energy dissipation, 1 - Fq'/Fm' - phiNO.
          The actively regulated, protective share of the absorbed light.
npq.t     NPQ(t): NPQ estimated without a dark-adapted Fm reference, so it can be
          computed from light-adapted measurements alone.
ChlIdx    Chlorophyll index, a reflectance index tracking chlorophyll content.
AriIdx    Anthocyanin reflectance index, tracking anthocyanin accumulation.
NDVI      Normalised difference vegetation index, a reflectance index of green
          canopy density and vigour.

The first eleven are chlorophyll-fluorescence traits from the PSII protocol;
ChlIdx, AriIdx and NDVI are multispectral reflectance indices; PSA is a size
measurement from the image mask.


HOW IT WAS CALCULATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unit of replication
    The maternal line (Population, A-K). Every regression is fitted on
    maternal-line means, never on individual plants. At each timepoint a group
    contributes 15 points (5 SWHC regimes x 3 lines), or 10 for Oregon 2014.

The regression
    At each timepoint, within each population group and for each trait:
    lm(trait ~ SWHC) across those line means. SWHC is the nominal watering
    regime, 20 to 100% of the soil water-holding-capacity reference weight,
    treated as a continuous predictor. A negative slope therefore means the trait
    falls as the pots get wetter, i.e. rises under drought.

What is plotted
    Fill is sign(slope) x -log10(P): the direction of the response in the colour
    (green positive, purple negative) and the strength of the evidence in the
    saturation, on a diverging scale centred on zero. The slope itself cannot be
    put on one colour scale across the 14 traits, since they carry different
    units - PSA is an area in a.u., Fv/Fm is a ratio between 0 and 1 - so the
    sign is taken from the slope and the magnitude from a quantity that is
    comparable across traits. Setting FILL_MODE at the top of the script to
    "signed_r" fills with sign(slope) x sqrt(R2) instead, which shows effect
    size rather than strength of evidence, and "logp" reproduces the earlier
    unsigned -log10(P) version of this figure.
    Red asterisks mark cells passing Benjamini-Hochberg at FDR < 0.05, corrected
    across the 52 timepoints within each trait x group - the natural family for
    "at which timepoints does this trait respond in this group".

Time
    52 timepoints on a 12 h grid, 144 to 747 h. The series starts at 144 h, the
    minimum of projected shoot area in all four groups. Early = 144-408 h
    (experiment 20243), Late = 411-747 h (experiment 20244); these are separate
    plant cohorts, which is what the dashed ring marks.

Inset trait and timepoint
    The trait is set by PANEL_A_TRAIT at the top of the script, currently NDVI.
    The timepoint is chosen by the script, not by hand: the first timepoint at
    which that trait's regression reaches P < 0.05 in at least one group. For
    NDVI that is the very first timepoint of the series, 144 h. The script
    prints it as "Inset timepoint" when it runs; trust that over any value
    quoted here. Set N_GROUPS_REQUIRED at the top of the script to 2 to require
    corroboration by a second group instead. The
    timepoint is selected once, from the line means, and used for both versions
    of the figure, so that the replication unit is the only thing that differs
    between them.

Inset geometry
    Each inset is a square of side INSET_SIZE (0.19) as a fraction of the ring's
    panel, placed in the corner its quadrant points into. coord_polar forces a
    square panel, so the ring is a circle inscribed in that square and the four
    corners are empty. The ring's drawn content, trait labels included, reaches
    0.86 of the panel's half-width, i.e. a radius of 0.43 panel sides, so a
    corner square first touches it at sqrt(2) x (0.5 - s) = 0.43, s = 0.196.

Quadrant names
    Centred on each quadrant's own block of traits, at GROUP_LABEL_R
    (1.16 x max_toe), just outside the trait labels. NOT in the gap wedge that
    closes a quadrant: that gap sits against the next quadrant's first trait, so
    a name placed there reads as belonging to that one.
    Set along the arc rather than horizontally. A tangential label keeps a
    constant radius, so it neither dips into the ring at its ends - which a
    horizontal label centred on the bisector does - nor runs out into the corner
    where the inset is. The angle is the trait labels' radial angle minus 90,
    folded into (-90, 90] so no name comes out upside down.

Pointing at the cell
    Three marks, all of them exact in polar coordinates: the cell's own red
    outline, the red dot-dash ring at its timepoint, and a red radial pointer
    from the centre out to the cell's inner edge. A line at constant angle is
    straight in polar coordinates, so the pointer lands on the cell by
    construction.
    There is no leader line from the inset to the cell. One was tried, drawn on a
    transparent overlay laid over the panel, and it assumed that overlay's 0-1
    box and the ring's drawn circle share a centre AND a radius. coord_polar
    forces the panel square, so as soon as the page is not exactly square the
    drawn circle is inset within the panel box and the two disagree: the line
    lands at the wrong radius, which reads as pointing at a different timepoint
    from the cell it belongs to.

Tile geometry
    geom_tile takes its height from the smallest spacing between TOE values. The
    grid is 12 h everywhere except across the run break (408 to 411 h = 3 h), so
    the height is set explicitly to the modal spacing; otherwise every tile is
    drawn a quarter of its proper size.


RESULTS, FOR THE TEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The insets, NDVI at 144 h on line means (slope per % SWHC, R2, P):

    California 2014   -0.00024   R2 = 0.43   P = 0.008
    Oregon 2014       -0.00037   R2 = 0.66   P = 0.004
    California 2010   -0.00048   R2 = 0.32   P = 0.028
    Oregon 2010       -0.00016   R2 = 0.21   P = 0.083

All four slopes are negative: NDVI is higher in the drier pots from the first
timepoint onwards, and three of four groups are already significant there.

Three things to draw out:

1. The groups differ enormously in how much of the trait panel responds at all.
   California 2014 is significant in 13 of 14 traits and accounts for half of all
   significant cells; Oregon 2010 responds in only four traits.

2. The reflectance indices are the most consistent responders. ChlIdx, AriIdx and
   NDVI are significant in all four groups or three of four, and ChlIdx reaches
   significance at every one of the 52 timepoints in California 2014.

3. Signs are consistent across every significant cell of a given trait. ChlIdx,
   AriIdx, NDVI, Fv/Fm, Fq'/Fm', qP and phiNO always have negative slopes - they
   are higher under drought - while PSA, NPQ, qN, qI, phiNPQ and npq.t always
   have positive slopes. qL never reaches significance anywhere.

CAVEATS WORTH KEEPING IN THE TEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Statistical power differs between groups. In the lineMeans version Oregon 2014
   has 2 maternal lines and so 10 points per regression against 15 for the
   others. Its lower count of significant cells is partly that, not only biology.
   The same imbalance carries into the plants version, where Oregon 2014 has
   two lines' worth of plants rather than three.

2. The input table is spline output on a 12 h grid with no Data.Support column,
   so the fits include smoothed as well as measured values. Smoothing shrinks the
   residual and the P values run optimistic. Adequate for ranking traits and
   comparing groups; not for quoting individual P values.

3. Many of these traits are not independent of one another - qP and qL, NPQ and
   qN, phiNPQ and npq.t measure overlapping quantities - so the quadrants should
   be read as a pattern across correlated measures rather than as 14 independent
   tests.

4. The BH correction is applied across timepoints within a trait x group, not
   across traits. It controls "at which timepoints does this trait respond", not
   "which traits respond".

5. The colour carries the sign of the slope but its magnitude is a P value, not
   an effect size. A deeply coloured cell means the response is well resolved at
   that timepoint, not that it is large. Where the size of the response matters
   for the text, quote the slope or R2 from
   data/Fig4_trait_vs_SWHC_lineMeans.csv, or rerun with FILL_MODE =
   "signed_r".


THE TWO VERSIONS OF THIS FIGURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This figure exists twice, and the two files differ in one thing only: what counts
as a replicate in every regression.

    Figure4_TreeRing_lineMeans.pdf  the maternal line is the replicate. 15 points
                                    per group per timepoint (5 SWHC regimes x 3
                                    lines), 10 for Oregon 2014.
    Figure4_TreeRing_plants.pdf     the individual plant is the replicate. 56 to
                                    112 points per group per timepoint.

Same traits, same model, same colour scale construction, same code - only n
changes. The title at the top of each page states which one it is, and the n of
each corner regression is printed in that inset.

Which to believe: the line-level version is the conservative one and is what
should carry any claim in the text. Plants within a maternal line are siblings
grown side by side, so they are not independent replicates of the line's
response; treating them as such inflates the degrees of freedom and shrinks the
P values without adding information about the line. Expect the plant-level ring
to be much more saturated and to carry far more asterisks. That difference is
mostly degrees of freedom, not newly discovered biology.

The plant-level version is still worth having. Because the colour carries the
sign of the slope, the two rings can be compared on direction rather than
significance: a trait whose sign is consistent between the two is responding
coherently, and a trait whose sign flips between them is being driven by a
single line or a few plants and should not be reported. That comparison is the
main reason this version is in the supplement.
