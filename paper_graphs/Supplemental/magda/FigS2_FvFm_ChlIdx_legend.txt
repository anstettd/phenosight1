DRAFT LEGEND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Figure S2. Chlorophyll fluorescence and pigment response of Mimulus
across a soil water-holding-capacity (SWHC) gradient, by collection site and
collection year. (A) Maximum quantum yield of photosystem II (Fv/Fm) over the
course of the experiment for each of five watering regimes (20, 40, 60, 80 and
100% SWHC), shown separately for populations collected in Oregon and southern
California in 2010 and 2014. Lines are means across maternal lines and shaded
bands are standard errors across maternal lines (n = 3 lines per site x year
group, except Oregon 2014, n = 2). Beneath each set of curves, -log10(P) for the
contrast between each watering regime and 100% SWHC at that time point, from a
one-way ANOVA across all five regimes followed by Tukey's HSD; the dotted
horizontal line marks P = 0.05. (B) As in (A), for the Chlorophyll Index. In both
panels the vertical dashed line at 409.5 h marks the point at which the plants
outgrew the imaging trays and the setup was changed; the discontinuity there is
technical and not biological.


HOW EACH QUANTITY WAS CALCULATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unit of replication
    Two versions of this figure, one per unit: the maternal line (Population,
    A-K), with every plotted value first reduced to a line mean, or the
    individual plant, with nothing pre-averaged. Identical to Figure S1 in both
    cases. Counts quoted below are the lineMeans version.

Time cut-off
    All series start at 144 h (TOE_MIN = 140), the minimum of projected shoot
    area in all four site x year groups. 52 time points on a 12 h grid:
    Early 144-408 h (23 points, experiment 20243) and Late 411-747 h (29 points,
    experiment 20244).

Fv/Fm
    Maximum quantum yield of PSII from the PhenoSight export, averaged over
    plants within a maternal line at each time point. Measured after in-machine
    dark adaptation, so values from the two daily rounds are comparable: the
    plant-level means differ by less than 0.001 between the 02:00 and 14:00
    rounds of the early interval.

Chlorophyll Index
    The ChlIdx channel of the PhenoSight export, reduced to maternal-line means
    the same way.

Statistics (both panels)
    At each time point, within each site x year group: one-way ANOVA across the
    five regimes on maternal-line means, followed by Tukey's HSD. Only the four
    contrasts against 100% SWHC are plotted. The value shown is -log10 of
    Tukey's adjusted P, so the four comparisons made at that time point are
    already accounted for. The omnibus ANOVA P is kept in the output tables as
    P_anova.

Scaling
    Within each panel, all four trait sub-panels share one y axis and both
    -log10(P) sub-panels share another, so groups are directly comparable.
    Panels A and B are scaled independently of one another.

RESULTS, FOR THE TEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Time points (of 52) at which a regime differs from 100% SWHC, Tukey P < 0.05:

  Fv/Fm                 20%   40%   60%   80%     omnibus ANOVA P<0.05
    Oregon 2010          19     0     0     0            24
    Oregon 2014          13     0     0     0            18
    California 2010       6     0     0     0            14
    California 2014      18     0     0     0            27

  Chlorophyll Index     20%   40%   60%   80%     omnibus ANOVA P<0.05
    Oregon 2010          17     4     5     0            18
    Oregon 2014          18     1     1     0            14
    California 2010      25    12    30     0            34
    California 2014      49    49    50    10            52

Two things to draw out:

1. Fv/Fm responds only to the driest regime. 20% SWHC separates from 100% in
   every group, and 40, 60 and 80% never do, at any time point, in any group.
   It is a threshold response, not a graded one.

2. Chlorophyll Index is graded, and the grading is strongest in California 2014,
   where 20, 40 and 60% SWHC are each distinguishable from 100% at essentially
   every time point (49, 49 and 50 of 52) and even 80% separates at 10. The
   omnibus ANOVA is significant at all 52 time points in that group. The two
   Oregon groups show the threshold pattern instead, with only the 20% contrast
   appreciably significant.

So the pigment index carries a dose-response that Fv/Fm does not, and the
difference between the sites is larger for the pigment index than for Fv/Fm.
This is consistent with the idea that pigment accumulation adjusts progressively
with water availability while Fv/Fm is held stable until stress becomes severe.

Ranges, for reference: Fv/Fm line means span 0.746-0.809 (interval means 0.782
Early, 0.794 Late); Chlorophyll Index 1.412-2.413 (1.715 Early, 1.905 Late).

CAVEATS WORTH KEEPING IN THE TEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Statistical power. With three maternal lines per regime (two for Oregon 2014),
   Tukey's HSD needs a large difference to reach P < 0.05. Zero significant time
   points for the 40-80% contrasts in Fv/Fm is consistent with a true threshold
   response, but it is also what low replication would produce; the omnibus
   ANOVA being significant at 14-27 time points while no individual contrast
   except 20% reaches significance is the signature of exactly that tension.

2. The input tables are spline outputs on a 12 h grid and carry no Data.Support
   column, so the fits include smoothed as well as measured values. Smoothing
   shrinks the residual and the P values are correspondingly optimistic.

3. Fv/Fm spans a narrow range (0.746-0.809). Differences that are statistically
   detectable are physiologically small, and the shared y axis makes that
   honest — but it is worth stating the effect size in the text rather than
   relying on the figure alone.

4. The measurement rounds alternate between day and night (02:00/14:00 in the
   early interval, 05:00/17:00 in the late one). Fv/Fm is insensitive to this
   after in-machine dark adaptation, but the sawtooth visible in some series is
   partly diurnal rather than noise, and would matter for any trait compared
   across rather than within rounds.


THE TWO VERSIONS OF THIS FIGURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This figure exists twice, and the two files differ in one thing only: what counts
as a replicate.

    *_lineMeans.pdf   the maternal line is the replicate. Every plant is first
                      averaged into its line x regime x timepoint mean, so each
                      group contributes 3 points per regime (2 for Oregon 2014).
                      Built from data/PS2_all.csv and data/GR_all.csv.

    *_plants.pdf      the individual plant is the replicate. The same rows, not
                      averaged, so each group contributes 9-15 points per regime.
                      Built from data/PS2_all_plants.csv and data/GR_all_plants.csv.

Same traits, same models, same panels, same code - only n changes. The title at
the top of each page states which one it is.

Which to believe: the line-level version is the conservative one and is what
should carry any claim in the text. Plants within a maternal line are siblings
grown side by side, so they are not independent replicates of the line's
response; treating them as such inflates the degrees of freedom and shrinks the
P values without adding information about the line. The plant-level version is
here because it shows how much of the apparent signal is real structure and how
much is the line-level design simply not having the power to resolve it: a
contrast that is significant at plant level and absent at line level is a
candidate, not a result.

Read the pair this way. Where the two agree, the conclusion is solid. Where only
the plant-level version is significant, say so explicitly and attribute it to
power. Where only the line-level version is significant, something is going on at
the line level that the within-line variance swamps - worth checking which lines
drive it.
