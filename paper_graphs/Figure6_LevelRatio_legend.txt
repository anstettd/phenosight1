FIGURE 6 — Level difference between Mimulus collections
================================================================================
MAIN figure, companion to Figure 4. Contrast: California relative to Oregon,
within each collection year. Quantity: level ratio (California / Oregon) and how
it trends across the gradient.

Generated by paper_scripts/4.2_Fig5_6_Contrast_TreeRing_MMJ.R, which writes
eight figures in one run - two contrasts (Figure 5 by year, Figure 6 by site) x
two quantities x two replication units. This legend covers:

    Figure6_LevelRatio_lineMeans.pdf
    Figure6_LevelRatio_plants.pdf

Statistics for both are in data/Figure6_contrast_regressions_<unit>.csv, which
carries BOTH quantities and BOTH P values for every cell.
Input tables are written by paper_scripts/0_data_setup.R.
Written 12 Nov 2026 (MMJ).


DRAFT LEGEND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Figure 6. Level difference between Mimulus collections across a soil
water-holding-capacity (SWHC) gradient, shown for every trait at every
timepoint. Each cell of the half-ring is one contrast of California against
Oregon, for one trait at one timepoint, within one collection year. Radius is
time of experiment, with the centre at the start of the series and each
successive ring a later timepoint; angle is trait, grouped into one 90-degree
quadrant per collection year, with the traits in the same order in both
quadrants and named at the outer edge of each wedge. Quadrants are 2014 in the
upper right quadrant and 2010 in the upper left, so the older collection reads
on the left, named along the arc outside the trait labels. An index is formed as
mean(California) / mean(Oregon) within each watering regime, so a value above 1
means California sits higher than Oregon. That index is then regressed on %
SWHC, and fill is the SLOPE of that regression - how the ratio between the two
collections changes as the pots get wetter: green where the ratio rises with
added water, purple where it rises under drought, and near-white where it is
flat across the gradient. Red asterisks mark cells at which the Site term of
lm(trait ~ Site + SWHC) passes a Benjamini-Hochberg correction across timepoints
at FDR < 0.05, i.e. the two collections differ at all, holding watering
constant. Note that this tests the level difference and not the slope the colour
shows. The dashed arc at 409.5 h marks the point at which the plants outgrew the
imaging trays and the setup was changed; any step across it is technical, not
biological. Inset below each quadrant is the regression a single cell is made
of, for the trait and timepoint picked out in the ring by a red outline, a red
radial pointer from the centre and the red dot-dash arc. The lower half of the
figure carries no data: it holds the colour key and those two insets.


HOW IT WAS CALCULATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The quantity
    Within one quadrant, one trait, one timepoint and one watering regime, the
    mean of California is divided by the mean of Oregon. That gives one index
    value per watering regime - five of them - and the index is regressed on %
    SWHC. The cell carries that slope, not the index itself. Because the index
    is a ratio it is dimensionless, so its slope is comparable across traits
    without further standardising.

The test behind the asterisks
    The Site term of lm(trait ~ Site + SWHC): "do the two collections differ
    here at all", holding watering constant. Benjamini-Hochberg across the 52
    timepoints within each quadrant x trait. NOTE that this is NOT a test of
    what the colour shows. The formal test of the fill is the Site x SWHC
    interaction, which is in the CSV as P_con_swhc and is what the companion
    Delta-plasticity figure marks.

Time
    52 timepoints on a 12 h grid, 144 to 747 h. The series starts at 144 h, the
    minimum of projected shoot area in all four site x year groups. Early =
    144-408 h (experiment 20243), Late = 411-747 h (experiment 20244); these are
    separate plant cohorts, which is what the dashed arc marks. Nothing is
    fitted across that boundary - each cell belongs to one timepoint only.

The insets
    Below each quadrant, the index against % SWHC for the trait and timepoint
    marked in the ring: the five points the cell's regression is fitted to, the
    fitted line coloured by the sign of its slope on the ring's convention, and
    the slope, R2, the slope's own P and the Site P printed above. The dotted
    horizontal line is an index of 1, i.e. no difference.

Which timepoint the insets show
    Chosen by the script, not by hand: the first timepoint at which the marked P
    for INSET_TRAIT (currently NDVI) falls below 0.05 in at least one quadrant,
    taken from the LINE-MEAN version so that both replication versions of this
    figure illustrate the same cell. If no timepoint qualifies, the one with the
    smallest P is used and the script says so. The number is printed on the run.

Colour limits
    Symmetric about zero, set at the 98th percentile of |fill| (FILL_QUANTILE),
    with anything beyond squished to the end of the ramp. Without that a single
    unstable cell flattens the rest of the figure. The script reports how many
    cells were squished.

Tile and ring geometry
    geom_tile takes its height from the smallest spacing between timepoints. The
    grid is 12 h everywhere except across the run break (408 to 411 h = 3 h), so
    the height is set explicitly to the modal spacing; otherwise every tile is
    drawn a quarter of its proper size.

    The gridlines and the two reference rings are drawn as ARCS over the two
    occupied quadrants only. A horizontal line in a polar coordinate system
    sweeps the full 360 degrees, which would put them across the empty lower
    half and under the insets.

Read the counts with care. 1080 of 1456 is 74% of the panel, and the Site term
is fitted across 25-30 rows drawn from only 5 maternal lines, so the degrees of
freedom are optimistic even in the line-mean version. The two sites clearly
differ in trait level in a common garden; the count of significant cells is not
a measure of how much of the panel is divergent.

The companion Delta-plasticity figure finds no change in SLOPE at line level
anywhere, so the same reading applies as for Figure 5: divergence in level, no
resolvable divergence in plasticity.


THE TWO VERSIONS OF THIS FIGURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two files, differing in one thing only: what counts as a replicate.

    Figure6_LevelRatio_lineMeans.pdf
        the maternal line is the replicate.
    Figure6_LevelRatio_plants.pdf
        the individual plant is the replicate.

Same contrast, same models, same colour construction, same code - only n
changes. The title at the top of each page states which one it is.

The line-level version is the conservative one and is what should carry any
claim in the text. Plants within a maternal line are siblings grown side by
side, so they are not independent replicates of the line's response; treating
them as such inflates the degrees of freedom and shrinks the P values without
adding information about the line. Expect the plant-level figure to carry far
more asterisks, and read that as degrees of freedom rather than as newly
discovered biology.

The plant-level version still earns its place: because the colour carries a
direction, the two can be compared on SIGN rather than on significance. A cell
whose sign agrees between the two is responding coherently; one that flips is
driven by a single line or a few plants and should not be reported.


CAVEATS WORTH KEEPING IN THE TEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The contrast is confounded with line sampling.

   "Site" is confounded with line sampling in the same way, and the two sites
   are not equally replicated in both years: Oregon 2014 has two maternal lines
   against three everywhere else, so the 2014 quadrant rests on a thinner Oregon
   sample than the 2010 quadrant does. Since the plants are grown in a common
   garden, a site difference here is a difference between the collections, not
   between the field environments they came from.

2. Each slope is fitted to FIVE points, one per watering regime, so it has three
   degrees of freedom. The slopes are reasonable descriptors; their own P values
   are weak, which is part of why the asterisks come from the Site test instead.

   A ratio of means is unstable wherever the denominator approaches zero, and
   several of these traits legitimately sit near zero at some timepoints (phiNO,
   qI, NPQ early on). Cells whose Oregon mean is under MIN_DENOM_FRAC (0.05) of
   that trait's own typical magnitude are dropped rather than drawn as a spike;
   the script reports how many went and from which traits. Setting INDEX_SCALE
   to "log2" regresses log2 of the ratio instead, which is symmetric in the two
   directions and better behaved, at the cost of needing both means positive.

3. Statistical power is uneven between quadrants. The quadrant containing Oregon
   2014 rests on two maternal lines against three elsewhere, so a difference in
   how much of its trait panel lights up is partly power, not only biology.

4. The input tables are spline output on a 12 h grid with no Data.Support
   column, so the fits include smoothed as well as measured values. Smoothing
   shrinks the residual and the P values run optimistic. Adequate for ranking
   traits and comparing quadrants; not for quoting an individual P.

5. Many of these traits are not independent of one another - qP and qL, NPQ and
   qN, phiNPQ and npq.t measure overlapping quantities - so a quadrant should be
   read as a pattern across correlated measures rather than as 14 independent
   tests.

6. The Benjamini-Hochberg correction is applied across timepoints within a
   quadrant x trait, not across traits. It controls "at which timepoints does
   this contrast hold for this trait", not "which traits differ".

7. Radius is experimental time, so a wedge that is near-white at the centre and
   deepens outward is a difference that BUILDS as the experiment runs. One that
   is uniformly deep from the centre out was already there at the first image,
   which points to a level or germination difference rather than to a response
   acquired under the treatment.
